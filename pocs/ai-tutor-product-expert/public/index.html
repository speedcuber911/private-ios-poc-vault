Crisp read-off pitch

Complex enterprise software is expensive to explain, and that cost shows up directly in stalled deals, slow onboarding, and repeated support questions.

$1.4T+ Gartner forecast for worldwide software spending in 2026.
$299B Cloud application services, or SaaS, forecast for 2025.
$240B Information security spending estimate for 2026.
$1.3B-$5.4B Estimated direct TAM if an AI product-education layer captures 0.25%-1.0% of SaaS plus security spend; cloud, DevTool, data, observability, and IAM expand the upside.

This is not a small workflow automation problem. It sits on top of large software, cloud, cybersecurity, and AI budgets where product complexity directly affects revenue.

Forrester says 86% of B2B purchases stall, and the average buying decision now involves 13 people across multiple departments. Gartner says buyers spend only 17% of the buying journey meeting suppliers. If three vendors are being evaluated, each vendor may get only 5% to 6% of the buyer's time.

Today, the answers are scattered across docs, PDFs, help centers, demo decks, sales engineers, support teams, and customer success calls. Salesforce says reps spend only 28% of their time selling. Software sales engineers are often well over $130K a year before full loaded cost. Gartner benchmarks assisted support at $13.50 per contact, compared with $1.84 for self-service.

The problem is simple: product knowledge is critical to revenue, but it is still delivered manually.

We are solving this with an AI Product Expert built on top of our existing AI Tutor platform.

AI Tutor already teaches complex concepts through conversation, step-by-step flows, visual explanations, interactive Q&A, prompt configuration, session tracking, and analytics. We can repurpose that foundation from academic tutoring to enterprise product education.

The product becomes an AI sales engineer, onboarding tutor, and product expert. It can explain workflows, architecture, integrations, SSO, RBAC, security, compliance, APIs, onboarding steps, and customer-specific use cases. It adapts the answer for a CTO, CISO, developer, admin, sales rep, or business user.

This is not a generic chatbot or AI documentation. It is a product knowledge layer that makes complex software easier to buy, onboard, and support.

The value is practical: reduce repetitive sales engineering load, speed up customer onboarding, deflect recurring support questions, and show product and GTM teams exactly where buyers and customers are confused.

The first wedge is one complex module: SSO and permissions, developer onboarding, security review, admin setup, or integrations. Turn that into an interactive AI product expert, prove the reduction in repeated questions and manual explanation, then expand across the product.
